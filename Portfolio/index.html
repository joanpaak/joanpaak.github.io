Science related projects

Master's thesis: generalizing Bayesian adaptive estimation

In my master's thesis I researched adaptive estimation, that is, the choice of covariates in a statistical model in optimal way. This technique is widely used e.g. in psychophysics where the intensity of a test tone is adapted to a participant's responses in order to quickly determine their hearing threshold.

In my theoretical study I showed that the existing Bayesian adaptive estimatation methods can be generalized to e.g. simulation-based models. This generalized version is applicable to wide variety of contexts, far beyond the usual applications of adaptive testing. In the practical section of my thesis I show how to apply it to estimation of auditory filters (ROEX) and to a drift-diffusion model of reaction times.

Novel algorithm for finding the prime form of a pitch class set

In the musical set theory pitch-class sets are often abstracted to their prime form, that is, into the most "compact" form. These are easily found by usign visual methods, but computationally finding them is usually based on rather brute forceish methods.

Here I show how to use a simple matrix-based method for finding prime froms of pitch class sets. This method is fast and easy to implement in all common programming languages.

JavaScript Implementation of a Ranking Task

I have implemented several psychophysical tasks in a few programming languages. Here I showcase my implementation of a ranking task for estimating detection thresholds for frequency differences.

The task itself is implemented in JavaScript, but the package includes a Stan program for doing Bayesian inference for a Signal Detection Theory based model of ranking as well as R scripts for analyzing the data and doing posterior predictive checks

Bachelor's thesis: separability of pitch and timbre in sensorimotor synchronization

Does timbre (in this case: brightness) of a sound influence how we perceive its pitch and vice versa? This question has been probed with traditional psyhcophysical methods, but here I wanted to develop a methdod based on measuring how well participants are able to synchronize their tapping to stimuli that have congruent or incongruent metrical sructures as implied by the stimuli's pitch and timbre.

The aim was to develop measurements that would free the participant from the constraints of a standard rather passive psychophysical task.

Tone-Row Fiddle for 12-tone Composition

Sometimes I analyze or compose 12-tone music. I programmed this tool in JavaScript to help me in these hobbies. You can input your tone row, which does not need to be a 12-tone row, and the application will give you the 12-tone matrix. That's standard stuff, but you an also input a row fragment and the application will highlight the rows which contain that fragment — making it faster to match pitch sets to rows.

As a bonus, for more general analysis of atonal music, there is a prime form finder, which implements the algorithm that is mentioned among the Science related projects above.
